Rotor Dynamic Mechanics, Delamination Acoustics, & Quadratic Inversion

Detailed mathematical formulation governing high-speed carbon-fiber reinforced polymer (CFRP) flywheels in Integrated Power and Attitude Control Systems (IPACS).

1. Centripetal Force Scaling & Quadratic Baseline

In an ideal rigid rotor supported by active magnetic bearings (AMBs), dynamic radial bearing force is governed by the center-of-mass eccentricity $\epsilon$ relative to the magnetic axis of rotation. Because linear velocity at the outer radius $r_o$ is $v = \omega r_o$, the centripetal bearing load scales strictly with the square of velocity:

$$\mathbf{F}_{\text{radial}}(\omega) = m_{\text{rotor}} \cdot \epsilon \cdot \omega^2 = \frac{m_{\text{rotor}} \epsilon}{r_o^2} v^2$$

Where $m_{\text{rotor}}$ is total rotor mass, $\epsilon = \|\mathbf{r}_{\text{c.o.m.}} - \mathbf{r}_{\text{bearing}}\|$ is the radial mass center eccentricity offset, and $\omega$ is rotational velocity in rad/s. In an intact, perfectly balanced rotor, $\epsilon$ remains constant or expands smoothly due to elastic Poisson compliance:

$$\epsilon(\omega) = \epsilon_0 + k_{\text{compliance}} \omega^2$$
2. Carbon-Fiber Rim Micro-Delamination & Mass Asymmetry

Carbon-fiber composite rims (such as Toray T1000G with toughened epoxy) operate at rim surface velocities exceeding $1,100\text{ m/s}$. Micro-void formation, epoxy debonding, or transverse matrix cracking causes local density variations and radial fiber unwinding:

$$\Delta \mathbf{F}_{\text{anomaly}} = \sum_{k=1}^{N} \delta m_k r_k \omega^2 \sin(\omega t + \phi_k)$$

When a delamination event of mass $\delta m$ separates by a minute distance $\delta r$ toward the containment ring, the center of mass moves discontinuously by $\Delta \epsilon = \frac{\delta m}{M} \delta r$. This introduces a sudden harmonic force deviation that violates the baseline parabolic trajectory:

$$\Gamma_{\text{fault}} = \left| \frac{dF_{\text{meas}}}{dv} - 2 k_{\text{nom}} v \right| > \kappa_{\text{trip}}$$
3. Real-Time State Observer & Inversion Filter

To distinguish benign thermal expansion from structural debonding, the onboard digital signal processor executes an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) paired with a high-bandwidth $(100\text{ kHz})$ recursive least-squares (RLS) estimator on the active magnetic bearing current telemetry:

$$\mathbf{i}_{\text{coil}}(t) = k_x x(t) + k_i^{-1} F_{\text{bearing}}(t) + \mathbf{w}(t)$$

The estimator isolates the synchronous 1X vibration component from 2X/4X aerodynamic or motor slotting harmonics. If the normalized quadratic residual exceeds statistical thresholds ($3\sigma$), an automatic emergency regen command is dispatched:

$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{residual}} = \frac{F_{\text{measured}} - \alpha v^2 - \beta v^4}{\sigma_{\text{sensor}}} \ge 3.0$$
4. Fast Sub-Cycle Dynamic Braking & Regen Inverter

Stored mechanical kinetic energy is $E_k = \frac{1}{2} J \omega^2$. Conventional mechanical friction brakes are vaporized at $60,000\text{ RPM}$. FlywheelSpeed utilizes the permanent-magnet synchronous motor/generator (PMSM) stator connected to a bidirectional silicon carbide (SiC) MOSFET bridge:

$$P_{\text{bleed}}(t) = \tau_{\text{regen}} \cdot \omega(t) = \frac{3}{2} p \left( \lambda_{pm} i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q \right) \omega(t)$$

By slamming the quadrature current $i_q$ to maximum reverse saturation in under $4.2\text{ ms}$, kinetic energy is decelerated safely into the host satellite bus or industrial DC microgrid, collapsing rotor hoop stress below catastrophic failure criteria:

$$\sigma_{\text{hoop}} = \rho_{\text{CFRP}} r_o^2 \omega^2 \le \sigma_{\text{allowable}}$$
